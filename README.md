# Stephen Ambrose Beck - National Medal

In August of 2026 researchers from the Korean broadcaster MBC (Munhwa
Broadcasting Corporation) contacted Brent Benson Sr. and Lois Hodik as
to whether they were children of Katherine Beck Benson and
grandchildren of Stephen Ambrose Beck. Stephen Beck, who died in 1941,
had been posthumously awarded the Order of Merit for National
Foundation, National Medal (건국훈장 애족장) in 2015 for his support
and dedication to the Korean independence movement while Korea was
under Japanese rule in the early 20th century.

They confirmed that they were descendants of Stephen Beck and provided
some photos and documentation of their mother's memories of smuggling
documents describing the situation in Korea back to the United
States. Lois was subsequently interviewed for a televised MBC news
story.

## The award

The Korean government's own record of the award is reproduced, in
translation and in the Korean original, in
[merit-record.md](merit-record.md).

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Recipient | Stephen Ambrose Beck (스티븐 앰브로즈 벡), 1866-12-09 to 1941-11-21 |
| Korean names used | 白瑞岩 (Baek Seo-am), 裵額 (Bae Aek) |
| Award | 건국훈장 애족장, the Order of Merit for National Foundation, National Medal (fifth class) |
| Awarded | 2015, posthumously, in the 70th-anniversary-of-liberation group of ten foreign recipients |
| Category | Support of the independence movement (독립운동지원) |
| Ministry record no. | 955752 |

In brief, the citation credits Beck with:

- accompanying the British consul, foreign correspondents and other
  missionaries to the site of the Jeam-ri church massacre in April 1919,
  and exhibiting photographs of it at a church exposition in Miami that
  July;
- delivering those photographs to Senator George W. Norris of Nebraska,
  who then raised the matter on the floor of the U.S. Senate;
- smuggling March 1st Movement documents out of Korea hidden in his
  luggage, including papers of the Hansong Provisional Government, in
  May 1919, and a second set received from Yi Sang-jae in November 1919
  that he delivered to Syngman Rhee;
- lecturing at more than 200 places across the United States between May
  and September 1919;
- serving as a propagandist for the Korean Commission to America and
  Europe from September 1919, and as general secretary of the League of
  the Friends of Korea and of the Korean Relief Society in 1920, in which
  capacity he submitted a "Resolution on Korea" to Secretary of State
  Bainbridge Colby urging American intervention.

Before Korea, and between his two tours there, Beck was a Methodist
Episcopal missionary: he arrived in November 1899, ran the Methodist
publishing house in Seoul and served as secretary and treasurer of the
Korea annual conference until 1908, then returned in May 1911 as the
American Bible Society's general secretary for Korea, a post he resigned
in December 1919.
