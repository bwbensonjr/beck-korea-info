## Anchor

We reported recently that MBC had located the descendants of three foreign
merit recipients whose medals the government had not presented for 11 years,
on the grounds that their descendants could not be found. Now MBC has found
the descendant of one more.

This recipient was an American missionary whose entire family devoted
themselves to Korea's independence during the Japanese colonial period.

An exclusive report by reporter Jung Hye-in.

## Report

In 1919 came the "Jeam-ri massacre," in which Japanese troops gathered
residents who had taken part in the March 1st Movement into a church, killed
them, and set the building on fire.

Stephen Ambrose Beck, who had come from the United States as a missionary and
who visited the site, secretly carried photographs of the atrocity to the
United States and exhibited them there, and also delivered them to a U.S.
senator.

Afterwards he went on to act as a bridge linking independence activists at
home and abroad, for instance by delivering secret documents relating to the
independence movement inside Korea to former President Syngman Rhee, who was
then in the United States.

He was also appointed a propagandist of the Shanghai Provisional Government
and held hundreds of lectures in the United States exposing the atrocities of
imperial Japan. In recognition of this dedication to the independence
movement, he was posthumously awarded the Order of Merit for National
Foundation, National Medal (건국훈장 애족장), in 2015.

But for 11 years the medal went undelivered, on the grounds that his
descendants could not be found.

MBC tracked him through overseas genealogy record sites and obituaries, and
found his granddaughter in the United States.

The granddaughter, who said she had not known of the Korean government's
posthumous award, knew her grandfather's achievements well. Not only her
grandfather, she said, but the whole family had thrown themselves into the
Korean independence movement.

*Lois Benson Hodik / Granddaughter of the independence patriot*:
"My mother was five years old at the time, and (my grandparents, the
recipient and his wife) put documents inside my mother's doll. My grandmother
held her breath, they say, whenever Japanese officials searched the luggage."

News of the posthumous award, however belatedly it came, was a great gift to
a family that had given so much for Korea.

*Lois Benson Hodik*:
"That my mother remembered so vividly the ship's cabin being searched (by
Japanese soldiers), and how terrifying that was... If she had known that all
his efforts were finally recognized, she would have been truly happy."

Of the eight foreign independence patriots whose medals the government has not
presented because their descendants were unknown, MBC has now located the
descendants of four.

MBC plans to pass the materials found this time to the Ministry of Patriots
and Veterans Affairs as well, so that the descendants can receive the medal.

This is Jung Hye-in, MBC News.

## Translation notes

Corrections made to the earlier English version in this repository:

- **Headline.** "후손 미상" means "descendants unknown," not "unknown
  daughters." The Korean headline also carries the count "4 of 8," which the
  earlier English dropped.
- **"Anchor," not "Abstract."** The section marker ◀ 앵커 ▶ introduces the
  studio anchor's lead-in, not a summary of the piece.
- **Beck and the photographs.** The Korean reads 참사 사진을 몰래 미국으로
  가져가 전시하고 - "secretly *carried* photographs of the atrocity to the
  United States and exhibited them." The earlier English, "secretly took
  photographs of the tragedy back to the U.S.," could be read as saying Beck
  photographed the scene himself. The broadcast does not say that, and neither
  does the government citation; see [merit-record.md](merit-record.md).
- **Name of the medal.** 건국훈장 애족장 is the Order of Merit for National
  Foundation, National Medal - the fifth class. "Patriotic Chapter" in the
  earlier version was a machine-translation artifact. (The fourth class,
  애국장, is the Patriotic Medal.)
- **Tense in the second quotation.** The Korean is past tense throughout -
  기억했다는 것 ("that she remembered") and 아셨다면 ("if she had known").
  Katherine Beck Benson had died by the time of the broadcast, so the earlier
  "My mother vividly remembers" and "If she knew" were wrong.
- **선실 is a ship's cabin**, a stateroom aboard a vessel, not a cabin on land.
- **Speaker caption.** The on-screen caption reads 독립유공자 손녀,
  "granddaughter of the independence patriot," rather than naming Beck.

Caveats that remain:

- **The quotations are back-translations.** Lois Benson Hodik spoke in
  English; MBC broadcast her words rendered into Korean, and this text renders
  that Korean back into English. Her actual wording will differ. Her own
  account is the authority here, not this text.
- **"Shanghai Provisional Government"** is what the broadcast says. The
  government's own citation instead names the Korean Commission to America and
  Europe (구미위원부), Syngman Rhee's Washington body, and dates the
  appointment to September 1919. See
  [research-notes.md](research-notes.md).
- **"Former President Syngman Rhee"** follows Korean press convention of using
  a person's highest later title. In 1919 Rhee held no such office.
