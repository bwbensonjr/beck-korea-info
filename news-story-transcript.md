# MBC Finds Descendants of More Foreign Meritorious Service Personnel with Unknown Daughters

## Abstract 

We recently reported that MBC located the descendants of three foreign nationals whose medals had not been presented to them for 11 years because the government claimed it could not locate them. Now,

MBC has found the descendant of yet another individual.

This individual is an American missionary whose entire family dedicated themselves to Korea's independence during the Japanese colonial period.

This is an exclusive report by reporter Jung Hye-in.

## Report

In 1919, the "Jeam-ri Massacre" occurred, in which Japanese troops gathered residents who had participated in the March 1st Movement in a church, murdered them, and set fire to the building.

Stephen Ambrose Beck, who was a missionary from the United States at the time, visited the site and secretly took photographs of the tragedy back to the U.S. to exhibit, and also presented them to a U.S. Senator.

Subsequently, he served as a bridge connecting independence activists at home and abroad, such as delivering secret documents related to the domestic independence movement to former President Syngman Rhee, who was based in the United States.

Appointed as a propagandist for the Provisional Government of Shanghai, he held hundreds of lectures in the U.S. to publicize the atrocities of Imperial Japan. In recognition of his dedication to the independence movement, he was posthumously awarded the Order of Merit for National Foundation, Patriotic Chapter, in 2015.

However, for 11 years, the medal could not be presented because his descendants could not be located.

MBC tracked down his granddaughter in the United States by tracing overseas genealogical records and obituaries.

Although she was unaware of the Korean government's posthumous award, the granddaughter was well aware of her grandfather's contributions. She explained that at the time, not only her grandfather but the entire family had devoted themselves to the Korean independence movement together.

*Lois Benson Hodik / Granddaughter of Stephen Ambrose Beck*: 
"My mother was five years old at the time, and (my grandparents) put documents inside her doll. My grandmother said she held her breath whenever Japanese officials searched the luggage."


The news of the posthumous awarding of medals, learned belatedly, was a great gift to the family who dedicated themselves to Korea.

*Lois Benson Hodik*: 
"My mother vividly remembers the cabin being searched (by the Japanese military) and how terrifying it was... If she knew that all his efforts had finally been recognized, she would have been truly happy."

Among the eight foreign independence patriots whose descendants were unknown and for whom the government did not present medals, MBC has so far located the descendants of four.

MBC plans to submit the materials discovered this time to the Ministry of Patriots and Veterans Affairs so that the descendants can receive the medals.
