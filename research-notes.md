# Research Notes and Context

Working notes assembled 2026-08-31 from Korean government databases and
published sources, to support the family's contact with the Ministry of
Patriots and Veterans Affairs. See [merit-record.md](merit-record.md) for the
official citation itself.

## Who and what the citation refers to

**Jeam-ri incident (제암리사건), 15 April 1919.** Japanese troops herded
villagers of Jeam-ri, Suwon County (today Hwaseong, Gyeonggi Province) into the
local Methodist church, shot them, and burned the building, in reprisal for
local participation in the March 1st Movement. Foreign missionaries and
diplomats who reached the site made the incident known outside Korea. The
memorial hall now on the site is listed in the Ministry's database as the
memorial associated with Beck.

**Hansong Provisional Government (한성정부).** The provisional government
proclaimed in Seoul in April 1919, one of three rival provisional bodies later
merged into the Provisional Government in Shanghai. Its founding documents were
among the papers Beck carried out of Korea.

**Sin Hung-u (신흥우, 申興雨; Hugh Cynn, 1883-1959).** Korean YMCA leader and
Methodist layman, active in the independence movement in the United States;
recipient of the Beck-carried Hansong Provisional Government papers.

**Yi Sang-jae (이상재, 李商在, 1850-1927).** Senior nationalist leader and YMCA
figure in Seoul, who gave Beck March 1st Movement documents in November 1919.

**Korean Commission to America and Europe (구미위원부).** Syngman Rhee's
diplomatic body, established in Washington in 1919 to lobby the U.S. government
and public. Beck was appointed one of its propagandists (선전원) in September
1919. Note that the MBC broadcast describes him as a propagandist for the
Shanghai Provisional Government; the official record specifically names the
Korean Commission, which operated out of Washington and Philadelphia.

**League of the Friends of Korea (한국친우회).** The pro-Korean advocacy
organization founded in Philadelphia in 1919 by Philip Jaisohn (Sŏ Chae-p'il),
with chapters in some 21 American cities plus London and Paris. Beck served as
its general secretary in 1920.

**Korean Relief Society (한인구제회).** American relief body for Koreans, also
organized in Philadelphia; Beck was likewise its general secretary.

**Senator George W. Norris (1861-1944).** Republican senator from Nebraska.
Beck was a Nebraska Wesleyan graduate, which plausibly explains the connection.
Norris was himself posthumously awarded the Order of Merit for National
Foundation in the same 2015 group as Beck.

**Secretary of State Bainbridge Colby.** Recipient, in March 1920, of the
Washington ministers' association "Resolution on Korea" that Beck submitted
over his signature as general secretary of the League of the Friends of Korea.

## The 2015 cohort of foreign recipients

Ten foreign nationals were honored in 2015, the 70th anniversary of liberation.
Filtering the Ministry's merit database by award year 2015 and foreign
nationality (국적: 외국인) returns exactly this list:

Spencer, Norris and Thomas were sitting U.S. senators; Tomkins was the
Philadelphia rector who chaired the League of the Friends of Korea. The group
as a whole reflects the 1919-1920 American advocacy campaign in which Beck
worked.

Cross-checking the same year against the Ministry's separate "searching for
descendants of independence patriots" registry
([독립유공자 후손찾기](https://e-gonghun.mpva.go.kr/user/RewardDisList.do))
shows eight of these ten still listed as having unidentified descendants:
Story, Marin, Guthapfel, Spencer, **Beck**, Cromwell, Thomas and Whiting.
Norris and Tomkins are not listed. That count of eight matches the "eight
foreign independence patriots whose descendants were unknown" figure MBC used
in its reporting.

Beck is also entry no. 5, marked with a star for undelivered honors, on the
Ministry's English-language
[F4K Series list](https://www.mpva.go.kr/english/contents.do?key=2014) of 76
foreign figures. The per-person English profile page for Beck exists at
`https://www.mpva.go.kr/english/contents.do?key=2019` but returned HTTP 403 to
requests from a U.S. address on 2026-08-31; it may need to be viewed from
within Korea or requested from the Ministry directly.

## Points where the news story and the official record differ

These are worth reconciling before the family submits documentation.

1. **Who photographed Jeam-ri.** The MBC broadcast says Beck "secretly took
   photographs of the tragedy back to the U.S." The official record says he
   accompanied the British consul, foreign correspondents and missionaries to
   the site in April 1919, and that in July 1919 he exhibited photographs of
   the incident in the United States. It does not say he was the photographer.
   The best-known Jeam-ri photographs are generally credited to the Canadian
   missionary Frank W. Schofield. Whether Beck took his own images, carried
   Schofield's, or both, is an open question.

2. **Which government he represented.** MBC says the Shanghai Provisional
   Government; the record says the Korean Commission to America and Europe.

3. **Name of the medal.** MBC's English subtitles say "Order of Merit for
   National Foundation, Patriotic Chapter." The award is the 애족장, the fifth
   class, officially the **National Medal**. (The fourth class, 애국장, is the
   Patriotic Medal.)

4. **Number of lectures.** MBC says "hundreds of lectures"; the record says
   more than 200 venues between May and September 1919.

5. **The doll story.** Lois Benson Hodik's account has her mother, then five
   years old, carrying documents hidden in her doll. Katherine Beck would have
   been about five in 1919, which fits the record's two documented smuggling
   runs: May 1919 (Hansong Provisional Government papers) and November 1919
   (papers from Yi Sang-jae for Syngman Rhee). The November trip is the one
   where Beck left Korea with his family after resigning from the American
   Bible Society, so it is the likelier occasion, but the record does not say.

## Open questions and archival leads

- **Death date.** The Ministry records 1941-11-21. A user-submitted Ancestry
  tree for a Stephen Ambrose Beck with the identical birth date of 1866-12-09
  (born Otterville, Jersey County, Illinois) gives a death of 1927-11-20 in
  Edgar, Clay County, Nebraska. The Nebraska connection is suggestive given
  Nebraska Wesleyan, but the two dates cannot both be right. Family records
  should settle it, and a correction can be filed through the merit database's
  own error-report form (오류신고) if needed.

- **"맥밀리언대학."** The Book of Merit says Beck graduated from this college
  before Nebraska Wesleyan. The transliteration reads as "McMillan," but no
  such Methodist college is obvious; McKendree College in Lebanon, Illinois is
  a candidate worth checking given his Illinois birth. Original transcripts
  would confirm.

- **American Bible Society archives.** Beck was ABS general secretary for Korea
  from May 1911 to December 1919. ABS annual reports and correspondence from
  that period are the most likely place to find his own account of April-May
  1919, and are held by the American Bible Society archives.

- **Methodist Episcopal Church records.** Beck ran the Methodist publishing
  house in Seoul and was secretary and treasurer of the Korea annual
  conference, 1899-1908. The annual conference minutes and *The Korea Mission
  Field* (digitized on the Internet Archive) should carry his name.

- **Sinhan Minbo (신한민보 / "The New Korea").** The Book of Merit cites four
  specific issues: 1919-07-10, 1919-08-19, 1920-05-11, 1920-11-25. These are
  contemporaneous Korean-American press coverage of Beck's lecture tour and
  organizational work.

- **Hong Seon-pyo's 2012 article** is the underlying scholarship for the 2015
  foreign awards; p. 197 covers Beck.
  [KCI record](https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART001724479)

- **League of the Friends of Korea papers.** A collection of "League of the
  Friends of Korea. Documents and correspondence, 1919-1920" is indexed on
  [Calisphere](https://calisphere.org/item/28daa46842d2c890fc0b73b7def18e52/);
  as general secretary in 1920, Beck should appear in it.

- **Congressional Record, July 1919.** The record states that Senator Norris
  spoke on the Senate floor in July 1919 after receiving Beck's photographs.
  The Congressional Record for that month would give the exact date and text.

- **"Church exposition in Miami," July 1919.** The Korean text is
  "미국 마이애미교회 박람회." Identifying the actual event, most likely a
  Methodist missionary exposition, would be a useful confirmation.
